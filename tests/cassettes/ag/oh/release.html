Wilson, Bipartisan Coalition Call CFTC’s Bluff on Sports-Betting Power Grab

11/18/2026

(COLUMBUS, Ohio) — A bipartisan coalition of 43 state attorneys general and the District of Columbia, co-led by Ohio Attorney General Andy Wilson is calling on federal regulators to withdraw a proposed rule intended to expand federal control over sports betting and undermine states' authority to protect consumers.

The attorneys general filed a formal comment with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), challenging its attempt to classify sports-related “event contracts” as financial instruments known as “swaps.”

“A sports bet isn’t a derivative just because the CFTC says so,” Wilson said. “The agency doesn't get to invent powers that Congress never gave it, ignore federal court decisions and bulldoze state consumer protections. If Congress wanted the commission running sports gambling, it would have said so — and headquartered it in Las Vegas.”

Prediction markets allow users to wager on sporting events much like traditional sportsbooks, but without the same state oversight. The proposed rule could further open the door for these platforms to bypass licensing requirements, age restrictions and protections against problem gambling.

The coalition points to federal appellate court decisions rejecting the CFTC's claimed authority over sports betting. The attorneys general also challenge the agency's ability to adopt the rule with only one commissioner currently serving, arguing that it lacks the required quorum.

The letter warns that the proposal could encourage traditional sportsbooks to reorganize as prediction markets, effectively sidestepping state gambling laws and leaving consumers without established safeguards.

The comment follows a July 2026 effort in which Ohio co-led a bipartisan coalition of 44

attorneys general urging the CFTC to recognize states' longstanding authority over sports-related prediction markets.

"The most straightforward answer is that sports bets are not derivatives subject to the CFTC's jurisdiction," the letter says.

The coalition urges the commission to withdraw the proposed rule and clarify that sports-betting contracts are not financial swaps subject to its jurisdiction, preserving states’ authority to regulate or prohibit sports gambling.

Wilson is co-leading the coalition alongside the attorneys general of Kentucky, Maryland, Nevada, New Jersey, New York, Tennessee and Utah. They are joined by 35 additional states and the District of Columbia.
