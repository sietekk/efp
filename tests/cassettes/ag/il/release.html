ATTORNEY GENERAL RAOUL, BIPARTISAN COALITION OF ATTORNEYS GENERAL OPPOSE RAIL MERGER

November 19, 2026

Chicago – Illinois Attorney General Kwame Raoul, as part of a bipartisan coalition of 21 attorneys general, urged the Surface Transportation Board to reject the proposed merger between Union Pacific Railroad Company and Norfolk Southern Railway Company.

In comments submitted to the Surface Transportation Board, which has sole jurisdiction to approve the merger, the coalition argues that the $85 billion merger will drastically reduce competition and harm the economies of the signatory states. In creating the largest private rail system in American history, the merger will adversely affect railway service, short-line rail and labor markets.

“Illinois is one of the nation’s major rail hubs, and this proposed merger would affect lines through much of the state,” Raoul said. “Rail service helps drive local economies, and it is important that there be healthy competition to help keep costs fair and ensure effective safety standards. I join this bipartisan group of my fellow attorneys general in calling on the Surface Transportation Board to reject this proposed merger.”

Raoul and the attorneys general assert that the merger fails to meet the board’s merger standard of requiring the merging parties to show “substantial and demonstrable public benefits to the transaction that cannot otherwise be achieved.” The coalition argues that Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern have not offered sufficient proposals that would remedy the harms to competition and the adverse effects on trading partners and the public that are likely to result from the merger. Instead of giving proper assurances to those most at risk of harm due to the merger, the CEO of Union Pacific has made threats to raise the prices for shipping customers who do not support the merger.

Given these concerns, Raoul and the attorneys general urge the board to consider the impacts on rail customers, laborers, local economies and end consumers of shipped goods, and deny the parties’ application to merge.

Raoul was joined in submitting the comments by the attorneys general of Arizona, Colorado, the District of Columbia, Florida, Kansas, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, North Carolina, North Dakota, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Nevada, Oregon, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Virginia, Washington and Wisconsin.
